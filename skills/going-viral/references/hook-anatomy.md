# Hook Anatomy

## What a hook actually does

A hook does two things in the first 1–3 seconds: (1) makes the viewer's thumb stop scrolling, (2) tells them *what they get* if they keep watching. If only (1) lands, you get a bait-and-switch flop. If only (2) lands, scroll past.

## Per-platform hook windows

- **TikTok**: 1.5–3 seconds. Audio + visual hook expected to land together. Text overlay is optional but strong.
- **Reels**: 2–4 seconds. Slightly more forgiving — IG's pacing is calmer. Text overlay almost mandatory because most viewers watch muted by default.
- **Shorts**: 1–2 seconds. Hardest window. YouTube viewers come from a search/recommendation context, are pickier about value-per-second.

## The 5 hook types that work

1. **Stake** — "I just lost $40k testing this so you don't have to." Promises the cost-of-experiment is already paid.
2. **Contradiction** — "Stop journaling. It's making your anxiety worse." Pattern interrupt against a held belief.
3. **Specificity** — "Here are the 3 emails that got me 17 sales calls last month." A number plus a category is sharper than the topic alone.
4. **Identification** — "If you've ever opened your closet and felt instant rage..." Names a specific feeling the viewer has had.
5. **Voyeurism** — "What a ten-figure investor's day actually looks like." Promises access.

## Failure modes

- **Vague identification**: "Everyone has this problem" — no, that's a generic statement. "If you've stared at your laptop at 11pm wondering why you took on this client" — that's identification.
- **Promise without proof of payoff**: "Watch this to fix your sleep forever" gives no preview of what the answer will be. Viewers bail.
- **Hook is a question**: questions feel like ads. "Are you struggling with X?" — scroll past. Statements outperform questions ~3:1 in our data.
- **Hook is the conclusion**: telling the punchline up-front kills retention. The hook should *promise* a payoff, not deliver it.
- **Stacking 3+ hook types**: feels desperate. Pick one.

## Hook → retention correlation

A strong hook gets you past the 3-second drop-off, but doesn't save a weak middle. Watch retention curves to diagnose:

- **Cliff at 3s**: hook is too vague. Viewer didn't get the promise.
- **Cliff at 8–12s**: hook delivered but the body didn't pay off the implied promise. Rewrite the second beat.
- **Steady fall, no cliff**: hook is fine, content is just slow. Tighten edits.
- **Curve goes UP at 15–20s**: the real hook is buried. Move that moment to the front.

## When to kill a hook

- After 5 attempts on the same hook variant pulling below your median, abandon it.
- A hook that worked once and fails three times after isn't a "format" — it was a one-off.
- Trend hooks (sound-driven) decay fast. The 3rd post on a trend is usually the last one that works.

## The promise-of-story hook

The strongest hooks raise an unanswered question — "what / why / how could this happen?" — a *promise of story*, not a flat statement of fact. "Africa is splitting in two" out-pulls "the world's strangest animals" because it implies stakes and a story coming. Frame the opener so the viewer has to keep watching to resolve it, then actually pay it off (see [narrative-tension-and-retention](./narrative-tension-and-retention.md)).

## The first 3 seconds are the highest-leverage fix

When a video underperforms, the opening is almost always the cheapest, biggest lever — a weak first 3 seconds can be the gap between 1M and 20M on the same body. Diagnose with view rate and the retention curve before re-cutting anything else (see [self-analysis-and-iteration](./self-analysis-and-iteration.md)).

## Title to the benefit, not the topic

Name what the viewer *gets*, not the category: "How I Bought a Tesla for $78/Month" beats "car-financing tips." A generic, overused topic can be revived purely by reframing it with a sharp, quantified, stakes-bearing hook (see [content-ideation-pipeline](./content-ideation-pipeline.md)).

## What NOT to do

- Don't start with a face just standing there. Either be doing something (specific action) or open with text.
- Don't open on b-roll. The first frame should communicate.
- Don't lead with brand identity ("Hi, I'm X from Y"). Save it for the end if at all.
- Don't reuse a hook that worked verbatim. Rephrase. Algorithms downrank duplicates.
