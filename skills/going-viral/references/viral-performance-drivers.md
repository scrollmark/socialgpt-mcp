# Viral Performance Drivers

A performance driver is a specific element that measurably moves views, watch-time, or engagement. These are the recurring *upward* drivers worth deliberately building in, and the *downward* drivers worth cutting. Treat them as a detection kit while researching (see [reverse-engineering-outliers](./reverse-engineering-outliers.md)) and as a build checklist — not an exhaustive list. Tension is its own driver; it lives in [narrative-tension-and-retention](./narrative-tension-and-retention.md).

## Upward drivers

1. **Cleverness** — *unexpected + smart*. Link two unrelated ideas so coherently the viewer thinks "why didn't I think of that?" Visual metaphors on tired topics, witty reframings, a mundane object repurposed. Hard to teach, easy to recognize; when present it almost always reads as a win.
2. **Absurdity** — unexpected, ridiculous, or oddly specific. Novelty makes it hard to look away and offers escapism. Engineer moments where a subject reacts *before* they understand what's happening — that captures unfiltered, genuine personality. Needs a real payoff or the curiosity it opens curdles.
3. **Perspective shift** — introduce a widely held belief, then subvert it. Debunk a misconception or invert the expected outcome so the viewer reevaluates something they thought they knew. The target reaction: "wow, I had that backwards."
4. **Viewer connection** — make the content feel one-to-one. Speak to "you" (singular), look into the lens, frame from the average viewer's point of view, and predict their internal monologue. Deliver either *actionable value* ("glad I watched that") or *educational insight* that satisfies curiosity. Spell out the **implications** so it feels personally consequential, not just interesting.

## Downward drivers (cut these)

- **Overt branding** — prominent logos, rigid palettes, brand-first framing. Viewers sense an agenda and bail; nobody opens the app wanting an ad. Prove a format works minimally branded, then add branding subtly and measure the hit (see "Brand last" below).
- **Production-value worship** — resolution, lighting, and audio polish don't cause success. iPhone-shot, compelling content routinely beats beautiful, empty montages. (Remember ~60% watch muted — a gorgeous sound-off edit that's hard to follow dies.)
- **Stock imagery** — reads as corporate and inauthentic. Acceptable only inside a larger piece or used self-aware/ironically. Prefer original visuals.
- **Grid-curation / standardized look** — meticulously consistent feeds optimize the profile page but feel disjointed in-feed and underperform. Optimize for the single post in the feed, not the grid.

## Brand last

Introduce branding only *after* a format is proven to travel. Make minimally branded content first; confirm it can go viral; then add branding and watch the numbers. If a logo causes a sharp engagement drop, it's detrimental — find subtler ways to include what's essential.

## How to use them

- While researching: for each Gold video, name which driver(s) are firing and which feeling they produce (the EOV).
- While building: aim to land 2–3 drivers, not all of them. Cleverness + absurdity pair well; perspective shift + viewer connection pair well.
- Cross-link: broad relatability comes from [generalist-principle](./generalist-principle.md); the retention driver (tension) from [narrative-tension-and-retention](./narrative-tension-and-retention.md).

## What NOT to do

- Don't bolt on a tactic because a winner used it — add it only if it produces the intended EOV.
- Don't chase "different" for its own sake; chase drivers that demonstrably move the metric.
- Don't model luck- or personality-driven hits — they aren't reproducible. Build on structure and concept instead.
