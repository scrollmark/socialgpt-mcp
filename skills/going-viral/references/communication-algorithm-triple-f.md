# Communication Algorithm (Triple F)

How you say something usually matters more than what you say. People process the world through six communication styles, and we default to our own — which is why a message written in your natural voice often reaches only ~30% of an audience. The fix is to consciously blend the styles most people share and drop the two that alienate. Pairs with [generalist-principle](./generalist-principle.md) (broad framing) and [viral-performance-drivers](./viral-performance-drivers.md) (viewer connection).

## The six styles (and the Big Three)

Roughly: **Feelings ~30%**, **Facts ~25%**, **Fun ~20%**, then Values/Opinions ~10%, Imagination ~10%, Action ~5%. The first three — the **Triple F** — cover ~75% of people. Build for them.

Match the audience's phrase signatures when you can:
- **Feelings** — emotion/relationship language: "I feel…", "I care", "we're close". Warm tone, empathy, eye contact, open body language.
- **Facts** — logic/data language: "I think", "the data shows", who/what/when/where. Give enough information to let them conclude for themselves.
- **Fun** — reaction language: "Wow", "this is awesome", "I love it". Energy, playfulness, props. (Fun ≠ jokes — even serious topics get "fun" through tactile, satisfying visuals.)

## Triple F in one piece

You don't need a separate video per type — blend all three in one. A clean mental model is three verbs:
- **Describe** (Feelings) — warm, sensory framing.
- **Explain** (Facts) — data, references, logical structure.
- **Demonstrate** (Fun) — props, energy, satisfying visuals.

A flat facts-only message ("4BR/3BA, 2,300 sq ft") covers a sliver of the audience; layering describe + explain + demonstrate widens it toward ~75%.

## Rule of non-values-based language

Values/opinion language resonates with only ~10% and invites counter-argument and division. Strip "should", "ought", "need", "we believe", "best on the market". Recast each into Feelings, Facts, or Fun, or reframe as a question. "Something must be done about X" → "I'm worried about X" (Feelings) / "Want the latest data on X?" (Facts) / "This X thing is wild" (Fun). Leading with one value repels everyone who prioritizes a competing value.

## Rule of non-autocratic language

~85% of people resist being told what to do, so commands ("Buy now or miss out!") shrink your audience. Convert commands into questions or warm invitations:
- **Democratic** (ask) — "Want to check out our best offer?"
- **Benevolent** (warm) — "We've got an offer for you."
- **Laissez-faire** (playful) — "Our offers are the bomb right now."
Earn the CTA by delivering value first, then make it the natural next step.

## A demographics warning

Two people identical on demographics (age, income, location) can have opposite communication styles. Match the *style*, not the persona sheet — an action-first "act now" can alienate a feelings-driven audience that looks demographically perfect.

## What NOT to do

- Don't write only in your own dominant style.
- Don't lean on values or commands for mass content — each alienates the large majority.
- Don't start sentences with "you" in fact-led copy; it shifts burden onto the listener — move "you" to the middle or end.
- Don't overdo any single mode; the subtlety of the blend is what makes it work.
