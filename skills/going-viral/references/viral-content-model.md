# Viral Content Model

Virality is closer to a science than a lottery. The creators who hit consistently aren't luckier — they run a loop: study what already works, form a hypothesis about *why*, test it with one post, and feed the result back in. This skill is that loop. It's the operating system the other skills plug into.

## The loop (five stages)

1. **Research** — find creators/formats in (or adjacent to) your niche that go viral *repeatedly*, not once. See [reverse-engineering-outliers](./reverse-engineering-outliers.md) for the method.
2. **Format selection + analysis** — pick one promising format and study its top, middle, and bottom performers to isolate the real drivers (not the surface features).
3. **Data-driven ideation** — generate many ideas grounded in those drivers, then rank and cut hard. See [content-ideation-pipeline](./content-ideation-pipeline.md).
4. **Single-iteration production** — make and ship *one* post. Not a batch. One post is one clean hypothesis test.
5. **Review + new hypothesis** — read the result against a known top performer, update what you believe, loop. See [self-analysis-and-iteration](./self-analysis-and-iteration.md).

## Why one-at-a-time beats batching

The default workflow — brainstorm a month of ideas, batch-shoot them, schedule, review at quarter-end — is the thing to kill. It produces strings of underperformers with no chance to learn between them. By the time you review, you can't tell whether a miss was a bad *idea* or bad *execution*. Analyze one post deeply, extract the lesson, and let it shape the next. Quality compounds; volume doesn't.

## Treat every assumption as a hypothesis

"Cute puppies go viral" is a trap — only the top sliver of puppy videos pop. Anything you believe about why content works is a hypothesis until a Gold-vs-Bronze comparison survives it. Hold beliefs loosely and try to break them fast.

## Reading a flop

- Post matched a known Gold reference on every driver but still flopped → your **research/hypothesis** was wrong. Go back to stage 1.
- Post missed drivers the Gold reference had → **execution** problem. Same idea, tighter build.
- Don't reflexively blame the idea or the algorithm; diagnose which stage failed.

## What NOT to do

- Don't prefill a content calendar weeks out — it locks you out of learning.
- Don't chase trends as your backbone; trends decay. Build on durable formats (see [viral-format-engineering](./viral-format-engineering.md)).
- Don't optimize for posting frequency. Master one working format first; *then* scale output.
- Don't start from a blank page when proven references exist — originality for its own sake throws away free data.
