# Generalist Principle

On social you don't compete only with others in your category — you compete with *every* piece of content fighting for the same scarce attention: baking, beauty, travel, drama, all of it. Most of the eligible content pool never gets shown. So winning only within your vertical isn't enough; the content has to be framed around broad human interest. This is the single most repeated lever for repeatable reach. "My niche is too boring/technical" is the mistake, not the reality.

## The move: reframe, don't water down

Keep the expertise intact; change the wrapper around it.

- **Lead with a universal angle, not the insider topic.** Graham Stephan turning car financing into "How I Bought a Tesla for $78/Month" — ~1,000 views became 8.5M. Same expertise, mass-appeal framing.
- **Pick broadly relevant entry points.** For a sleep doctor: sunlight, screen time, energy — things anyone cares about — rather than a sub-clinical sub-topic.
- **Assume zero prior knowledge.** Write for someone who's never heard your jargon. Define new terms. Test it with "would this interest my grandparent?"
- **Make it accessible without dumbing it down.** Newcomers should follow it; your core audience should still get the value underneath.

## Find your own proof

Look at your current top-performing post. It almost certainly won by appealing to the masses, not only your target demographic. That pattern is your template — replicate the framing that broke out, not the narrow ones that didn't.

## Why broad framing also feeds the business

Reach isn't the enemy of conversion — it feeds it. A modest conversion rate over hundreds of thousands of views beats a high rate over a thousand. Broad attention also builds the retargeting pool and top-of-mind awareness. Grab attention broadly, hold it with real substance, *then* monetize — in that order.

## Jargon is a wall

Industry terminology and marketing-speak signal "this isn't for you" to everyone outside the niche. Use the audience's own language (see [communication-algorithm-triple-f](./communication-algorithm-triple-f.md)). Open on a relatable, specific problem rather than your credentials — a credentials-first open reads as a sales pitch and turns viewers off.

## What NOT to do

- Don't hyper-target only your existing audience — it caps your ceiling. To grow, deliberately write for the unconverted.
- Don't assume an "unsexy" industry can't travel — tax, legal, terrarium-building, and hand surgery have all pulled millions with the right framing.
- Don't confuse "broad" with "generic." Broad framing is a sharp, specific story that happens to be universally relatable — not vague mass-market mush. Sharpen the hook with [hook-anatomy](./hook-anatomy.md).
