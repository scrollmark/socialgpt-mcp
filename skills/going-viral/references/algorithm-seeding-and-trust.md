# Algorithm Seeding & Trust

Every algorithm has one goal: keep people on the platform so it can serve more ads. It pursues that by testing your post on a small seed audience and expanding reach *only* if that audience engages. Understanding the seeding mechanic explains why posts stall, why new accounts start slow, and how to recover. Complements [platform-algorithm-fluency](./platform-algorithm-fluency.md) (per-platform signal weights) and [early-engagement-diagnostics](./early-engagement-diagnostics.md) (reading a fresh post's first hours).

## Seeding and the growth curve

Think of the algorithm as a cautious new friend who introduces you to a few people first, then to larger circles only as you earn their trust. A post is shown to ~5–10% of your typical reach, and expansion is gated on retention and engagement against everything else it could have shown that viewer.

- You don't *beat* the algorithm — you collaborate with it by out-retaining the next post in the feed.
- New accounts have no track record, so they get small seeds and must earn wider reach over repeated wins. Established creators (a MrBeast) get seeded to millions instantly because their history predicts engagement. Don't expect a beginner's reach to match a veteran's — that gap is trust, not a glitch.

## Trust is sticky, and rebuildable

- A post's fate is mostly decided early (around hour 6 on short-form); algorithm decisions there tend to stick.
- A slump is recoverable. Don't panic-delete one flop — deleting a single underperformer has no measurable effect (mass deletions can confuse the system; one-offs don't). Instead, study what your audience actually engaged with and ship a stronger, mass-appeal piece to re-earn reach.
- Consistently low-retention, cookie-cutter content erodes the algorithm's willingness to promote you over time. Bland content spends down trust.

## Paid amplifies; it can't manufacture

- Boosting works on content that *already* has organic appeal — validate organically first, then put spend behind a winner (especially to reignite something that plateaued but still drives leads).
- Your paid ad competes in the same feed against organic content, so it has to clear the same bar.
- Boosting content with no inherent appeal tends to fall flat and can even depress future organic reach. Paid is a multiplier on appeal, not a substitute for it.

## Spotting fake reach (engagement-ratio checks)

Big view counts with tiny likes usually mean paid inflation, not organic resonance — useful when deciding who to emulate (see [platform-algorithm-fluency](./platform-algorithm-fluency.md) for the per-platform like/view baselines). Don't model mega-brands whose reach comes from billions in off-platform ad spend and global recognition rather than content potency.

## What NOT to do

- Don't read a slow start on a new account as failure — it's the trust curve. Keep shipping quality.
- Don't delete single flops for the algorithm's sake; it's a myth.
- Don't pour paid spend into content you haven't validated organically.
- Don't assume a slump is permanent — diagnose, then re-earn trust with one strong post.
