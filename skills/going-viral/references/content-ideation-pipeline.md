# Content Ideation Pipeline

Good ideas are a numbers game with a filter on the end. Generate far more than you need, anchor each to a proven reference, then cut hard. This is stage 3 of the [viral-content-model](./viral-content-model.md) — the bridge between research and production. Pair with [hook-anatomy](./hook-anatomy.md) (sharpen the angle) and [reverse-engineering-outliers](./reverse-engineering-outliers.md) (where the references come from).

## Generate wide before you filter

- **Chaos first.** Welcome wild, "too crazy" ideas; pull from unrelated industries; don't self-censor in the generation phase. You can always tone down later.
- **Aim for volume — many ideas per keeper.** When you feel tapped out, keep pushing; the breakthroughs tend to surface right after the wall.
- Periodically audit the list for lopsidedness — too many in one direction, gaps in others.

## Log everything (the ideation sheet)

Keep a running sheet with four columns and revisit it as real performance comes in:
- **Rank (1–5)** — gut score, informed by what works on social.
- **Idea** — a one-to-two-sentence concept; bake the hook in here if you have one.
- **Reference link** — a high-performing video that backs the idea structurally.
- **Notes** — exactly what to replicate: a timestamp, a scene, the look/feel/structure.

Target 20+ ideas per session, each carrying a reference. No reference, weaker idea.

## Templatize Gold references

Turn a proven winner into a reusable skeleton: strip the topic-specific noun to a blank, then refill for your niche.
- "$1 vs $10,000,000 Yacht" → "$1 vs $10,000,000 ___" → your version.
- "How to Make Homemade Plant Food with 2 Ingredients" → "How to Make ___ with ___".
Drill it: original format+topic → same format, blank topic → your topic in the blank.

## Amplify a flat idea with a hook point

A generic topic (morning routines, productivity) can be revived by reframing it with a sharp, quantified, stakes-bearing angle — and titling to the *benefit*, not the category. "5 Ways to Be More Productive" → "How to Gain Back 15 Hours a Week." Title to the hook, not the topic.

## Rank ruthlessly (1 = best, 5 = worst)

- Give a **1** if it's detailed, backed by a strong reference, and easy to visualize with a clear narrative.
- Give a **5** if it's vague, hookless, or has no narrative. 2–4 = potential, needs tweaks.
- Prioritize the 1s (favor 1s with strong references when you're new), then workshop the top pick into variations.
- Stay flexible — drop a personal favorite for an idea with higher potential and fewer obstacles. Don't lock onto your first instinct.

## Say no a lot

- Replace "I don't like it" with the specific failure: "no value promise," "nothing to subvert," "no chance for tension."
- Kill team back-patting; reward critical questions.
- Avoid "good enough" — don't wing it when the research process can elevate it.

## What NOT to do

- Don't fall in love with the first idea; generate past it.
- Don't keep ideas with no reference and no hook.
- Don't treat rankings as permanent — re-score as data arrives.
- Don't let personal taste override what the references say works (see [reverse-engineering-outliers](./reverse-engineering-outliers.md)).
