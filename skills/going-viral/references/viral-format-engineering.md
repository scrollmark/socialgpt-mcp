# Viral Format Engineering

A **format** is a durable storytelling structure you pour any topic into — like film's three-act structure or music's verse-chorus. A **trend** is a fleeting moment (a sound, a meme) with a short shelf life. Formats last for years; trends decay in days. Build your channel on a format, borrow from trends opportunistically. For concrete blueprints see [proven-viral-formats](./proven-viral-formats.md); for the research method see [reverse-engineering-outliers](./reverse-engineering-outliers.md).

## Why master one format

Reinventing your structure every post is slow and prevents mastery. A format is a constraint that *frees* you — it removes the blank page so your energy goes into filling it well. Pick one top-performing format that genuinely fits you and run it until you're excellent, *then* consider scaling output or adding a second. Concentration drives growth; spreading thin dilutes it.

## Finding formats

**TikTok / YouTube** — keyword search and triangulate:
- Search topical terms, format terms, and even unrelated terms; use a model or trends tool to expand keywords.
- Combine a topical keyword with a format keyword: "Nutrition" + "Challenge", "Cooking" + "Hacks".
- General format keywords to mix in: hacks, challenge, tutorial, competition, skit, reaction, vlog, DIY, explainer, interview, man-on-the-street, "worth it", satisfying, versus.
- When a high-view video catches your eye, open the creator's profile and check whether they *repeat* the structure.

**Instagram** — search in-app, scroll to the Reels tab, eyeball view counts on the account's reels (no sort-by-performance), and confirm the format repeats.

## Validating a format is real (not a fluke)

- Require ~5–10 high-engagement examples of the format per creator, ideally across multiple creators.
- One viral hit is not a format — check for replication (see the consistency screen in [reverse-engineering-outliers](./reverse-engineering-outliers.md)).
- Account for different baselines: music formats run in the billions, fitness in the hundreds-of-thousands-to-millions. Judge within the format, never across.

## The three-act scaffold

Most durable formats map to three acts: **Act I** introduces characters/setup and ends on a pivotal event; **Act II** deepens the complication toward a turning point; **Act III** resolves it. Use the scaffold as a backbone and fill it with your flavor — variety comes from what you put *in* the structure, not from redesigning the structure.

## Knowing your lane

- Short-form ≈ 10s–3min (TikTok, Reels, Shorts).
- Long-form ≈ 8min+ (YouTube).
Pick a format suited to the lane and platform you're mastering first (see [viral-mindset-and-myths](./viral-mindset-and-myths.md) on winning one platform at a time).

## What NOT to do

- Don't build your backbone on trends — they expire.
- Don't run multiple formats before you've mastered one.
- Don't adopt a format off a single viral example; validate replication first.
- Don't treat "found a format" as the finish line — then you analyze its drivers deeply (see [proven-viral-formats](./proven-viral-formats.md), [viral-performance-drivers](./viral-performance-drivers.md)).
