# Proven Viral Formats

A format is a reusable storytelling structure you drop your topic into — film's three acts, not a one-off. These four are well-documented, work across niches, and each has a known driver set. Use them as starting blueprints; derive your own with [viral-format-engineering](./viral-format-engineering.md) and [reverse-engineering-outliers](./reverse-engineering-outliers.md). Master one before adding another.

## Visual Metaphor (short form)

Make an abstract idea concrete with a physical object plus a short verbal explanation. Great for "serious" niches (therapy, medicine, finance) that think they're too dry for short video.

Drivers (aim for all):
- **Active metaphor** — the object *demonstrates* the concept; never leave props static. (Dye spreading in a fish tank as a "toxic environment" — 20M+. Static cars-in-traffic as a metaphor — ~129K.)
- **Tension** — a looming, unresolved problem (a lit match burning toward fingers as she talks).
- **Potential energy** — in the opener, show the object holds energy about to release (a plate *and a hatchet* in frame) to seed anticipation.
- **Perspective shift** + **cleverness** — reframe a familiar idea in a way that earns "why didn't I think of it like that?"
- **Immediately applicable** — advice usable right now, not edge cases.

Flops: static/imaginary props, predictable reveals, an opener where the object isn't identifiable, edge-case advice.

## Two Characters, One Lightbulb (short form)

One creator plays both an expert and a clueless novice; a short exchange delivers a "lightbulb" that flips a common misconception. Ideal for myth-busting in fitness, law, real estate, finance.

Drivers:
- **Contrast** — exaggerate the gap between the two characters; fold in a real industry misconception so it's relatable.
- **Breaking misconceptions** — defy expectations; one clear "aha," not a list.
- **Digestible, actionable info** — plain language, no jargon dump; make the complex feel achievable.
- **Effective pacing** — get to the point fast; every line advances it; cut filler jokes.
- **Leave them wanting more** — give just enough, then a CTA — but *earn it first* (deliver value, then make the CTA the natural next step).

Flops: no real opposition between characters (no aha), jargon-heavy openers (Roth IRA / REIT cold opens → a few thousand views), impromptu CTAs that feel petty.

## Untold Stories (short form)

Take a familiar subject and reveal lesser-known "deep cuts" that trigger an "oh wait, what?!" — viewers feel like insiders.

Drivers:
- **Strong hook with the promise of a story** — within ~3s, promise hidden insight; anchor to a familiar subject so there's context.
- **The deep cut** — reveal gradually, each detail a stepping stone to the next surprise.
- **High, explicit stakes** — underdog or origin arcs; state what's on the line; withhold the life-changing payoff until the end.
- **Impactful conclusion** — resolve with a payoff worth sharing.

Flops: assuming the audience already has the backstory (sequels that skip the hook), visuals with no focal point, covering already-known outcomes, no stated stakes.

## 30-Day Challenge (long form)

Document a finite, relatable experiment ("I quit sugar for 30 days"). Balances one person's lived experience with universal appeal.

Drivers:
- **Accessible, actionable, novel topic** — anyone with willpower could try it; match duration to difficulty (too short reads fake, too long discourages); avoid dangerous stunts.
- **External research** — cheap credibility: an expert interview, a source book, a few charts, B-roll.
- **Lighting & composition** — 3D depth (sharp foreground, soft background), tight framing, intimate "talking to a friend" feel. No crew needed.
- **Storytelling / Jenga effect** — structure around obstacles, not chronology; stack twists (see [narrative-tension-and-retention](./narrative-tension-and-retention.md)).
- **Dynamic energy** — vary shots, captions, segments; show highs and lows so the viewer feels like a participant.

Flops: niche/inaccessible challenge, no research or visual aids, flat 2D framing, chronological structure with no tension, duration mismatched to difficulty.

## What NOT to do

- Don't run several formats at once — concentration drives mastery.
- Don't treat these four as the only options; they're examples. Find the format that fits *you* via research.
- Don't copy a format's surface; copy its driver set and the feeling it produces (see [viral-performance-drivers](./viral-performance-drivers.md)).
