# Narrative Tension & Retention

A hook earns the first 3 seconds; tension earns the next 30. Retention is the signal platforms reward hardest, and retention is an engineering problem: open a question the viewer needs answered, keep a new one open at all times, and pay it all off. This is the engine that makes a strong middle, not just a strong start. Pairs with [hook-anatomy](./hook-anatomy.md) (the open) and [story-structure-short-form](./story-structure-short-form.md) (the arc).

## The curiosity gap

Open a loop early — a question, a stake, a "wait, how?" — and *withhold the answer*. The unresolved gap is what keeps a thumb off the scroll. The cardinal sin is resolving it too early: tell the punchline up front and there's no reason to stay. Promise the payoff; don't deliver it yet.

## The Jenga method (stack tensions, don't run one thread)

A single thread of tension sags once it resolves. Instead, stack interconnected mini-tensions like Jenga blocks: as each small question resolves, a new one is already open. The outcome can even be predictable — the *layered* buildup is what holds attention, escalating to an amplified climax.

- Open with the central concept or a teaser of the big payoff.
- Introduce small, interconnected sub-questions rather than a straight line.
- Resolve one, immediately raise the next. Never let the last question close before the next opens.

Think shark video: do sharks smell blood? → how much? → human blood too? → now a dramatic dive. Each answer hands you the next question.

## Complete the scale

Tension demands resolution the way a musical scale demands its final note. Setup (hook → anticipation) must be matched by payoff (resolution) — subtle or dramatic, but *present*. An unresolved setup leaves the viewer unsettled and they won't share it. Sharing is a reach multiplier, so the payoff has to feel worth passing on.

## Techniques that manufacture anticipation

- **Teaser-of-the-ending**: show the final moment up front so viewers watch to see *how* you get there (the porch-pirate glitter-bomb move).
- **High, explicit stakes**: state what's on the line so the viewer anticipates a real win or loss. Unstated stakes read as flat.
- **Use the topic's own tension**: a controversial or bizarre subject can be the setup by itself.
- **Visual pull**: each shot should create a reason to watch the next one.

## Diagnosing where you lose them

Read the retention curve (see [self-analysis-and-iteration](./self-analysis-and-iteration.md)):

- Cliff at ~3s → the hook didn't land a clear promise.
- Cliff at ~8–12s → the gap opened but the next beat didn't pay off. Rewrite that beat.
- Slow steady decline → no open loop; content is just coasting. Add a tension.
- Curve ticks *up* mid-video → your real hook is buried there. Move it to the front.

## What NOT to do

- Don't front-load the conclusion; promise it, hold it.
- Don't stretch one tension past its natural end — stack new ones instead.
- Don't leave the loop unresolved to "build mystery"; an unpaid promise kills shares and rewatches.
- Don't confuse fast cutting with tension. Twenty cuts that add nothing still feel slow; pacing is information rate, not edit count (see [execution-craft-and-nuance](./execution-craft-and-nuance.md)).
