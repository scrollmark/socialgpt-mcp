# Story Structure (Short Form)

Tension keeps people watching (see [narrative-tension-and-retention](./narrative-tension-and-retention.md)); structure is what you hang the tension on. These are the scaffolds and devices for building a short piece that holds together from open to payoff. Pairs with [viral-performance-drivers](./viral-performance-drivers.md).

## The arc

Even a 30-second video benefits from a spine: intro → conflict → rising action → climax → resolution. Set the stakes early, escalate, pay off. Map longer pieces to three acts (setup → complication → resolution). The structure is a constraint that frees you — fill it with your flavor rather than reinventing it each time.

## One more layer

A single-dimension idea usually falls flat unless it's exceptional. Take a decent core concept and stack at least one more dimension on top:
- a format constraint (e.g. "5 levels of difficulty"),
- an audience-reaction layer (film real people reacting),
- a transformation or gimmick that becomes the through-line.
The layered transformation itself often becomes the hook that carries viewers end-to-end.

## The Golden Triangle (when you want depth)

For maximum resonance, interweave three ingredients: a **personal story** (relatability), **evidence/facts** (credibility), and a piece of **timeless wisdom** (universality). Not every piece needs all three, but the combination lands hard for thought-leadership and value content.

## Analogies: bridge the known to the new

Explain unfamiliar ideas by comparing them to something the audience already understands. Draft analogies with the "you know how…?" opener — if the listener nods along, it lands; if not, try another. Pivot the bridge toward the most universal benefit (taste over health, say) rather than the insider angle.

## Visualize complex topics simply

Turn abstract or dry subjects into clear, surprising visuals with physical or unexpected props. Lead with the intriguing *visual effect*, then unpack the logic — not facts-first. Work backward from "what unexpected visual could carry this?" (paper cutouts to explain tax brackets, for instance).

## Start from the effect, write backward

Decide the exact emotion you want first — awe, surprise, "I can't believe that's possible" — then build the piece to produce it. Viewers feel before they think, so the emotion is the design target. If you can't name a specific feeling you're going for, don't make the piece. Let the chosen emotion dictate the downstream choices.

## What NOT to do

- Don't ship a flat, single-layer idea and hope; add a layer or a clever angle first.
- Don't open complex topics with facts; open with the visual/effect, then explain.
- Don't start from the message and bolt emotion on after — start from the emotion.
- Don't mistake "different" for "good." Content is design (solving the retention problem), not art for its own sake — be good first, distinctive second (see [execution-craft-and-nuance](./execution-craft-and-nuance.md)).
