# Platform Algorithm Fluency

## Signal weight (rough order, per platform)

### TikTok
1. **Completion rate** (watched 100%) — dominant signal
2. **Re-watch rate** (loops) — second strongest
3. **Shares** — sends to a wider audience pool
4. **Comments** — quality matters; long-form replies count more than emoji
5. **Likes** — exists, but mostly as a tiebreaker
6. **Follow-from-post** — strongest *creator-level* signal but doesn't expand the post itself

### Reels (Instagram)
1. **Sends/Shares** — heaviest signal; saves are second
2. **Saves** — ~tied with sends, the "future value" signal
3. **Watch-time** — but Reels measures average view duration, not just completion
4. **Replays**
5. **Comments** — weighted lower than TikTok
6. **Likes** — least weight; mostly social proof for human viewers

### Shorts (YouTube)
1. **Average view duration** — dominant
2. **Confirmed engagement** (likes, comments) — surprisingly weighted higher than on Meta
3. **Click-through to long-form** — boosts the channel, not the Short
4. **Subscribes from Short** — strongest creator-level signal

## Save vs share vs comment

- **Save**: "I want this for myself later." Predicts future value. Best follow-conversion signal.
- **Share**: "Someone I know needs this." Drives reach but not always followers.
- **Comment**: "I want to be in conversation about this." Predicts engagement loyalty but not always reach.

A 1000-view post with 30 saves is healthier than a 5000-view post with 30 likes. Saves indicate the content is *useful*; likes are mostly social tax.

## Watch-time mechanics

- **TikTok**: completion is binary (did they finish or not). Re-watches *count again* toward total watch time, which is why looping content with sub-15s length wins.
- **Reels**: average view duration matters more than completion. A 90-second Reel with 60s avg can outperform a 30s Reel with 25s avg.
- **Shorts**: confirmed *intentional* watch (didn't bounce in <2s) is the threshold.

This means: short content (15–30s) wins on TikTok via re-watches; medium (45–90s) can win on Reels via duration; <60s is a hard cap on Shorts.

## Follow-conversion differences

- **TikTok**: lowest follow-conversion-per-view. Audience treats TikTok as broadcast media. Need to push profile clicks explicitly.
- **Reels**: medium. IG users still browse profiles before following.
- **Shorts**: highest. YouTube viewers follow the creator-as-channel mental model.

Implication: a creator getting 100k views on TikTok gaining 50 followers is normal. The same on Shorts should be 200+. If Shorts isn't converting, the channel positioning is the bottleneck, not the Short.

## Algorithm myths to ignore

- **"Post at 6pm Tuesday"**: posting time matters far less than first-hour engagement velocity. Post when *you* can babysit comments for the first hour, not when a generic chart says.
- **"Reply to every comment for the algorithm"**: replies don't directly boost the post. They boost the *comment's* visibility. Worth doing for community, not for reach.
- **"Hashtags drive reach"**: on TikTok and Reels, hashtags are weak categorization signals. Don't sweat them past 3–5 relevant tags.
- **"Don't delete posts, the algo punishes you"**: deleting a single flop has zero measurable effect. Mass deletions can confuse the recommendation system; one-offs don't.

## Engagement-ratio health checks

Like/view ratios separate organic resonance from paid-inflated vanity metrics: roughly **YouTube** likes ≥ ~1% of views, **TikTok** ~5–10%, and assume an **IG** post reaches only ~10% of followers. A big view count with very few likes usually flags paid boosting, not a format worth modeling. For how reach actually expands from a small seed — and how to rebuild it after a slump — see [algorithm-seeding-and-trust](./algorithm-seeding-and-trust.md).

## Cross-platform implications

- A piece of content that wins on one platform usually doesn't win on the others *as-is*. The hook framing, pacing, and length cap need adjustment.
- TikTok-native content reposted to Reels typically pulls 30–50% of its TikTok reach, not 100%. Don't read that as "Reels is dead."
- Shorts is the most format-strict — the same post on Shorts often pulls 10–20% of TikTok reach.
